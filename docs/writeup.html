The short version
  1. Problems 1 and 4 have the same fix. The daily rigour (timetable, 8pm quiz, focus time, chapter progress) lives in docs, folders and ClickUp today. If it moves into the app, students get a reason to open it every day, and the student dashboard fills itself. So I'd run the app-adoption work and the student-dashboard work as one project, not two.
  2. Make "30 minutes" mean 30 minutes of focus, not screen time. No parent wants their teen on a phone for longer. One 25-minute locked Focus session a day, plus the quiz and a day plan, gets a student to about 30 minutes, and every one of those minutes is time parents are glad about.
  3. Ship the GTM tracker first (live by 20 Nov). The data already exists in Meta, ClickUp and Razorpay, and it makes revenue accountable from week 3. The one thing to fix first is a lead ID that travels from the ad to the payment. Without it, nobody can say which campaign produced revenue, only which produced leads.
  4. Every feature or system goes through one loop: a one-page Bet Card with a metric, target and kill rule; tracking built before the feature; a 1-week pilot with one pod; and a readout on day 14 that decides scale, iterate or kill. Each step has an owner and a document. This is how I'd keep the success rate high with a team of three.

1. App adoption: 2.15 → 30 minutes a day

What the numbers say

  • The game works and study doesn't. Home and the Realm are used. The Focus Timer has 11 who tried it but only 5 regulars. Syllabus dropped from 10 students to 1, and nobody opened the Study Planner this week. Students come for the fun parts and leave before the parts that matter to us.
  • 25 features for 20 students is too much to think about. A 14-year-old opening the app has to choose between Home, the Realm, Dragons, Coins, Streak, Rankings, Side Quests, Syllabus, Planner, Goals, Wellness... Most of these got one try.
  • Wellness depends on hardware most students don't have. One student connected WHOOP. Most teens have an Apple Watch, a Samsung watch or nothing.
  • An average of 20 users is fragile. Two heavy Realm players can move it a lot. I'd track the median and the share of students who did the core action, not only the mean.

What I'd change the goal to

"30 minutes in the app" should mean "30 minutes of the app doing its job". The metric I'd make the north star is focus minutes per active Alcovian per day. It measures app usage and study effort at the same time, and it is screen time a parent is happy to see. Two supporting metrics: % of students who finish the daily loop (plan, focus, quiz), and 7-day return rate.

The plan: one daily loop instead of 25 features

StepTimeWhat changes
1. Plan my day~2 minAfter school, the app shows today's timetable blocks (tuition, sports, free time) and suggests 1–2 self-study blocks on chapters that are behind pace. One tap to accept.
2. Focus25–50 minStarts from the planned block. The phone locks except for whitelisted calls (parents). Focus minutes are the only way to win Realm territory and hatch dragons. Daily login coins drop. The game the students already like now pays for the study habit.
3. 8pm quiz~5 minMoves into the app with a push at 7:55pm, a pod leaderboard and streaks. It's an existing daily habit; we just move it to where the other data is.
4. Check-in~1 minMark chapter stage, rate today, see "my week". This feeds the student dashboard without anyone typing in ClickUp.
  • Hide, don't delete. Rankings, Side Quests, Goals and wellness extras move behind Discover until the loop works. Home becomes "your next step", not a feed of 9 cards.
  • Make it social. Pod focus rooms (5 Alcovians focusing at the same time) and a weekly pod focus goal. Pods already exist offline; this uses them.
  • Pull from advisors and parents. The weekly advisor review runs off the app's data, so advisors ask students to log. Parents get a Sunday summary ("Aanya focused 3h 40m this week"), so parents ask for it too.
  • Wellness with no effort. Read from Apple Health and Health Connect (Samsung, Garmin and others sync into these), plus WHOOP for students who have one. No new device needed.

Targets and how I'd test with 20 students

Ramp: 10 min by end of Dec → 20 by end of Feb → 30 by end of Mar. With 20 users, A/B tests won't reach significance. Instead I'd roll out pod by pod: one pod gets a change for a week, then compare against the other pods and against that pod's own previous week. I'd also watch 3 students use the app on a screen share for every release. With this few users, 3 live sessions teach more than a dashboard.

Guardrail: if focus minutes rise but quiz scores, chapter readiness or sleep drop, we're pushing too hard. Those are on the student dashboard for exactly this reason.

Three things that make it trustworthy

  1. One lead ID from ad to payment. Meta lead form → ClickUp task → Razorpay payment link notes all carry the same lead ID. This is the only way to judge a creative by the revenue it brought in, not the leads.
  2. ClickUp statuses are the funnel. One Leads list, one status per stage, and an n8n timestamp whenever a status changes. Nobody fills in the tracker by hand. Smallest.ai reminder calls are triggered when the status moves to "Discovery set", so the show-up rate is something we act on, not just report.
  3. Rules, not opinions, for RCA. Refresh if 7-day frequency is 3 or more or CTR is in the bottom quarter. Scale if CPQL is 20% or more below the campaign median and the creative has converted. Kill if spend passes 2× median CPQL with no qualified lead. We review the rules monthly; the tracker applies them daily.

Cadence: a 30-minute Monday GTM review using only the tracker. Each red number gets one owner and one action, logged in ClickUp.

3. A structure that makes features and systems succeed

Most features fail for one of three reasons: nobody agreed what success means, nobody can measure it after launch, or it adds steps for a teen who has no reason to take them. The process below targets those three. It's the same for an app feature, a new dashboard or an n8n automation.

StepOwnerDocumentThe step is done when
1. FrameMeBet Card (1 page): problem, who it's for (teen / parent / advisor), evidence, one success metric with baseline → target → date, one guardrail metric, kill rule, cognitive-load budgetThe founder agrees with the metric and the kill rule. If we can't name the metric, we don't build it
2. ShapeMe + systems designerFlow sketch, the events it must fire, manual steps it removes or adds, who does what30-minute review. The engineer can estimate it. Event names are added to the metric dictionary
3. BuildEngineerClickUp ticketsDone = works on the test account, events show up in Supabase, the dashboard tile exists, it's behind a flag
4. PilotMePilot notesOne pod for one week. I watch 3 students use it live. Bugs fixed
5. LaunchSystems designer3-line SOP for advisors, parent note if neededEveryone affected knows what changed and what they need to do
6. ReadoutMeDay-14 and day-30 readout in the decisions logDecision made: scale, iterate once, or kill. A missed target with no decision is not allowed

The cognitive-load budget (written on every Bet Card)

  • A teen can do the core action in 3 taps and under 10 seconds, with no explanation.
  • At most 1 new screen. If something is added, something else gets hidden.
  • A parent understands any summary we send in 5 seconds.
  • An advisor's manual input stays under 10 minutes per student per week.

Rhythm for a team of three

  • Monday (30 min): GTM review, then plan the week in ClickUp. At most 2 bets in build at once.
  • Daily: a written check-in in ClickUp (yesterday, today, blocked). No standing meeting.
  • Thursday: 20-minute demo of anything shipped or piloted.
  • Every other Friday: readouts and the decisions log.

Systems that don't break

  • Every n8n workflow has an owner, writes a row to ingest_log on each run, retries on failure, and alerts ClickUp on error. Upserts are keyed on source IDs, so a re-run never duplicates data.
  • A weekly data-quality check flags leads missing a stage date, payments with no lead ID, and students with no app event in 7 days.
  • Every system has a one-page runbook, so any of the three of us can fix it.

The advisor's job, given they aren't subject experts

The advisor doesn't need to teach chemistry. Their job is process: set targets from subject affinity and past scores, protect self-study time in the timetable, and check that each chapter goes through every stage of rigour (read → notes → practice ≥70% → timed past paper → ready ≥80%). When a subject falls a set percentage behind target, an alert brings in a tutor. The dashboard holds advisors to these process metrics, which they control, not to marks, which they don't.

What's in the repo

  • dashboard/: both dashboards (static HTML/JS, dummy data generated in the browser so filters really work)
  • sql/: Postgres/Supabase schema, views, and one metric query per dashboard tile
  • n8n/: importable workflows: ClickUp lead-stage sync (+ Smallest.ai reminders), Razorpay webhook, Meta Ads daily pull with creative alerts, nightly student roll-up and alerts, weekly advisor digest, and an error-alert workflow the others report failures to. The creative flags and student alert rules each live in one SQL function, so the dashboard and the alerts can never disagree

Assumptions

  • Usage numbers come from the brief; I haven't seen raw event data. Week 1 starts by confirming the baseline.
  • The 8pm quiz and the timetable currently run outside the app.
  • Supabase (Postgres) is used as the store because it's cheap, works well with n8n and the app, and lets a non-engineer read the SQL. Any Postgres would do.
